## 4. Voice Drift Audit Checklist

**Purpose:** Ensure that multi‑piece amplifications maintain voice consistency. Run this before scheduling any series of three or more pieces.

**Instructions:** Assemble all final drafts from the series into a single document. Read them in sequence, focusing solely on voice—not content. Use the checklist below to spot drift.

### Three‑Marker Check

**1. Hook Architecture**  
- Does every piece open with the specific hook pattern assigned in your planner (e.g., Bold Reframe, Vulnerability Ledger)?  
- Any piece that defaults to a generic opener (rhetorical question, “We’ve all…”, bland statement)?  
**Drift detected?** ☐ No ☐ Yes (list piece: _____________)

**2. Lexical Consistency**  
- Are your signature contractions and informal phrases present in all pieces? (e.g., “here’s the thing,” “not gonna lie”)  
- Did any piece slip into formal language, corporate jargon, or words on your banned list?  
**Drift detected?** ☐ No ☐ Yes (list piece and offending phrase: _____________)

**3. Emotional Register**  
- Does each piece’s emotional tone match its assigned role? (e.g., Day 2 should feel vulnerable, Day 3 instructive, Day 5 reflective)  
- Any piece that feels emotionally flat or, conversely, overwrought compared to the intended register?  
**Drift detected?** ☐ No ☐ Yes (list piece: _____________)

### Fixing Drift

If you answered “Yes” to any drift marker, do NOT rewrite the piece from scratch. Instead:
- Identify the specific prompt that generated the drifting piece.
- Add a reinforcing constraint to that prompt. For example: “Maintain the exact hook architecture from the Reference Example throughout. Do not vary.” Or, for long‑form pieces: “In outputs longer than 500 words, increase the frequency of contractions and direct reader address by 20% to compensate for formality drift.”
- Regenerate the piece and re‑audit.

Over time, add permanent “anti‑drift” rules to your prompt templates based on recurring patterns. E.g., “LONG‑FORM RULE: Every paragraph over 150 words must contain at least one contraction, one direct address to ‘you,’ and one short (under 8 words) punchy sentence.”
