## Tool 2: “Yes, And” Editorial Integration Worksheet

**Purpose:** To practice the improv‑inspired technique that transforms AI from a draft generator into a creative partner. Use this worksheet with any AI‑generated section that feels emotionally flat or safe.

**Instructions:** Take a passage from an AI draft (could be from a newsletter, carousel slide, or video script). Set a timer for 5 minutes. Follow the steps.

### Step 1: Accept the Offer

Read the AI passage without judgment. Instead of hunting for flaws, ask: “What’s the most interesting kernel here that could be deepened?” Write that kernel down.

**AI Passage:**  
[Paste passage]

**The Interesting Kernel (one sentence):**  
_______________

### Step 2: Add Your Unique Perspective

Write a raw, unpolished personal thought that builds on that kernel. It could be:
- A specific memory that contradicts the claim.
- A surprising implication the AI didn’t consider.
- A vulnerable admission (“Honestly, I still struggle with this.”)
- A question you’d ask if you were talking to a friend.

**My Raw Addition:**  
_______________

### Step 3: Rewrite Together

Feed the passage and your addition to the AI with the prompt:

*“Take the following AI-written paragraph and my additional thought. Rewrite the paragraph to integrate my thought naturally. Preserve the voice constraints I’ve given you. If my thought creates tension with the original, keep the tension—don’t resolve it.”*

**Output:**  
_______________

### Step 4: Reflect

After reading the integrated result, ask:
- Does it sound like I’m having a real conversation, not reciting a script?
- Did my personal addition survive the integration, or did the AI water it down? (If watered down, re‑run with a stronger instruction: “Keep my exact phrasing for the vulnerable admission.”)
- What did I learn about what the AI handles well vs. what needs my voice?

**Reflection Notes:**  
_______________

**Repeat this exercise weekly** with different types of content. Over time, the AI will learn to anticipate the depth you bring, and its first drafts will start incorporating more tension, humor, and vulnerability on their own.
