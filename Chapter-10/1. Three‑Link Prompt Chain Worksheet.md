## 1. Three‑Link Prompt Chain Worksheet

**Purpose:** Turn a Hub into a rich, 1,000‑word newsletter essay using a staged prompt sequence that inserts editorial control at every major step.

**Instructions:** Use this worksheet alongside a Hub that your Decision Matrix has rated highly (especially for Resonance and Evergreen Strength). Complete each link in order.

### Link 1 – Narrative Outline

**Prompt (copy into your AI tool):**

```
You are my editorial assistant. Using only the Hub content below, generate a narrative outline for a 1,000‑word newsletter essay.

The outline must include:
- An engaging personal opening (use the first Key Story as the seed)
- A section that deepens the Core Thesis with supporting data and a second story
- A section that addresses a major audience objection (use the Objections block)
- A reflective closing that ties the insight back to the reader’s experience

Do NOT write the full essay. Output only the outline with clear section headings.

Hub:
[PASTE YOUR FULL HUB]
```

**Outline review (before proceeding):**  
- Does the opening feel like it could be personal and hook the reader?  
- Is the thesis deepened, not just repeated?  
- Is the objection handled in a way that adds nuance, not just a strawman?  
- Would this outline make me want to read the full piece?

If you answered “no” to any question, adjust the outline manually or regenerate with a refined prompt. Once satisfied, move to Link 2.

### Link 2 – Voice‑Infused Draft

**Prompt:**

```
[PASTE YOUR VOICE CONSTRAINTS BLOCK FROM CHAPTER 5]

Using the outline below, write a full newsletter essay of approximately 1,000 words. Follow my voice rules exactly. Where the outline indicates a personal anecdote, invent a plausible placeholder story that matches my tone—I will replace it with a real story later. Add a contrarian opinion or unexpected insight in the third section.

Outline:
[PASTE THE APPROVED OUTLINE FROM LINK 1]

Output the full essay with the subject line at the top, prefixed by “Subject: ”.
```

**After receiving the draft:** Read it once for voice. Does the hook match your architecture? Are your signature phrases present? Mark any paragraph that feels generic or shallow. You’ll deepen those in Link 3.

### Link 3 – Editorial Deepening (“Yes, And”)

For 2–3 key paragraphs, write a raw, one‑sentence personal thought—a real memory, a contradictory reflection, or a vulnerable admission that only you could add. Then use this prompt for each paragraph:

```
Here is a paragraph from the AI‑written draft:
[PASTE PARAGRAPH]

Here is my additional thought that I want to integrate seamlessly:
[PASTE YOUR RAW SENTENCE]

Rewrite the paragraph so that my thought is woven in naturally, without breaking the voice or flow. If my thought challenges the original point, adjust the argument to hold the tension—don’t smooth it over.
```

**Final Polish:** Combine the deepened paragraphs into the full essay. Read aloud. Apply a final light edit for typos. Your newsletter is now voice‑deep and genuinely personal.

**Timebox goal:** 90 minutes total for all three links (including your thinking time between steps).
