## TOOL 2: The "Yes, And" Editorial Worksheet

**Purpose:** A structured worksheet for the improv-based deepening process. Helps you identify WHERE to intervene and WHAT to add, so you're not staring at a draft wondering "what's missing?"

**Time needed:** 15–20 minutes per piece (between Link 2 and Link 3)

---

### PIECE: _______________ FORMAT: _______________ DATE: _______________

---

#### Step 1: Read the Draft as a Collaborator, Not a Critic

Read the Link 2 draft once. Don't edit. Don't judge. Just read it the way you'd read a friend's first draft over coffee.

**My gut reaction (one sentence, honest):**

_______________________________________________________________

**The moment that felt most "mine":**

_______________________________________________________________

**The moment that felt most "AI":**

_______________________________________________________________

---

#### Step 2: Find the Three Intervention Points

Scan the draft for these three types of moments:

| Intervention Type | Where in the Draft? | What's Missing? |
|---|---|---|
| **The Lived Detail** — A spot where the AI wrote a plausible but generic scene. You have the REAL scene. The specific date. The exact words. The smell of the room. | Paragraph #__ | |
| **The Contrarian Aside** — A spot where the AI stated something "reasonable" that you actually disagree with, or that's more complicated than the draft lets on. | Paragraph #__ | |
| **The Vulnerable Admission** — A spot where the AI was "helpful" or "instructive" but you were actually scared, uncertain, or wrong. The draft is too clean. You were not clean. | Paragraph #__ | |

---

#### Step 3: Write Your Interventions (Raw, Unpolished)

Don't write "good prose." Write the way you'd talk to a friend. Fragments. Swear words. Half-finished thoughts. The AI will polish it. You just need to provide the *truth*.

**Intervention 1 (The Lived Detail):**

Target paragraph: "[Paste the paragraph]"

My real version:

"_______________________________________________________________
_______________________________________________________________
_______________________________________________________________"

*(Example: "The AI wrote 'I remember feeling overwhelmed during a busy week.' My real version: 'It was a Thursday. 4:47 p.m. I was sitting in my car in the Whole Foods parking lot because I'd driven there to buy dinner and then couldn't decide between the Thai place and the grocery store. I sat there for eleven minutes. Engine running. Hands on the steering wheel. Not crying. Just… stopped.'")*

---

**Intervention 2 (The Contrarian Aside):**

Target paragraph: "[Paste]"

My complication:

"_______________________________________________________________
_______________________________________________________________
_______________________________________________________________"

*(Example: "The AI wrote 'The framework helped me regain focus.' My complication: 'Honestly? The framework made me feel worse for about two weeks. Because it showed me how many decisions I'd been avoiding. The clarity was painful before it was liberating. Nobody talks about that part.'")*

---

**Intervention 3 (The Vulnerable Admission):**

Target paragraph: "[Paste]"

My admission:

"_______________________________________________________________
_______________________________________________________________
_______________________________________________________________"

*(Example: "The AI wrote 'I'm grateful for the lessons this experience taught me.' My admission: 'I'm not sure I'm grateful. I think I'm just… done being angry about it. And maybe that's enough. Maybe you don't have to be grateful for the thing that broke you. Maybe you just have to stop letting it drive.'")*

---

#### Step 4: Feed Into Link 3

Copy your three interventions into the Link 3 prompt (Tool 1). Run it. Read the integrated draft.

**Did the AI integrate them well?**

☐ Yes — the piece reads as one unified voice
☐ Mostly — one intervention feels slightly forced → Which: _______________
☐ No — the AI smoothed over my roughness → Re-run with: "Keep the rawness. Do NOT polish my addition into professional language."

**The final piece now feels:**

_______________________________________________________________

> *The "yes, and" process is where the AI stops being a tool and starts being a partner. You're not correcting it. You're building on its offer. And the piece that emerges is something neither of you could have made alone: structurally sound (the AI's contribution) and deeply, specifically, irreplaceably human (yours).*
