**Purpose:** The "final exam" of Chapter 10. Combine editorial depth, voice amplification, serialization, AND business strategy in a single integrated plan. This is where you prove you can wield the entire system with judgment, not just mechanics.

**Time needed:** 30 minutes

### The Challenge

Design a Five-Day Arc that serves BOTH editorial depth AND the business goal—without breaking trust, without feeling salesy, without exploiting the vulnerability that makes the content resonate.

---

### The Plan

| Day | Editorial Function | Business Function | How They Coexist |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 (Hook) | Build tension | Plant the seed of the problem your offer solves | The hook names the pain. No mention of the offer. The pain IS the relevance. |
| 2 (Story) | Vulnerability / connection | Build trust through shared experience | The story makes the reader feel less alone. Trust is the prerequisite for any future "yes." |
| 3 (Framework) | Teach / empower | Demonstrate expertise. Soft CTA. | The framework proves you know your stuff. The CTA is gentle: "I go deeper on this in [X]. No pressure." |
| 4 (Objection) | Intellectual honesty | Pre-empt the "this won't work for me" that prevents purchase | Address the objection that also prevents them from buying. "You might think [X]. Here's why that's not quite right." |
| 5 (Reflection) | Community / invitation | The invitation. The "door is open" moment. | The reflection invites conversation. The business CTA is the softest: "If you want to go deeper, here's where. If not, I'll see you in the replies." |

### The Day 3 Prompt (The Hardest One)

*Write the full prompt for the Framework piece, incorporating a gentle, non-salesy CTA that flows from the content.*

```
[Paste your full Day 3 prompt from Tool 6]

ADDITIONAL CONSTRAINT — BUSINESS INTEGRATION:
This piece is part of a Five-Day Arc that also serves as a
lead-generation pathway for [offer name].

THE RULE: The CTA must flow NATURALLY from the framework.
It must feel like a "by the way, if you want more" moment,
NOT a "and now I'm going to sell you something" pivot.

STRUCTURE FOR THE CTA:
- Deliver the full framework FIRST. Complete. Actionable. Free.
- The reader should be able to implement it WITHOUT buying anything.
- THEN, in the final 10% of the piece, add a gentle bridge:
  "If you want to go deeper on this—if you want the version
  where I [specific additional value], I do that in [offer].
  It's not for everyone. The framework above works whether
  you're in the room or not. But if [specific situation]
  resonates, here's where to find me: [link]."

NEVER:
- "Sign up now!"
- "Limited spots!"
- "Don't miss out!"
- Making the framework feel incomplete without the paid offer.
- Guilt-tripping. ("If you found this valuable, imagine how
  much more valuable [paid thing] would be.")

THE TEST: If a reader who will NEVER buy anything reads this
piece, do they still feel it was complete and generous? If yes,
the CTA is fine. If no, it's too aggressive. Pull back.
```

---

### The Trust Check

Before publishing the arc, ask:

☐ Does each day's content stand alone as valuable, even without the business goal?
☐ Is the CTA confined to Day 3 (and optionally a soft mention on Day 5)?
☐ Does the framework in Day 3 feel COMPLETE without the paid offer?
☐ Would I feel comfortable reading this arc if I were a skeptical reader who hates being sold to?
☐ Is the vulnerability in Day 2 serving the reader, or serving my sales funnel? (Be honest.)
☐ If I removed all CTAs, would the arc still work as pure editorial content?

**If any answer is "no":** Pull back the commercial element. The trust is worth more than the conversion. A reader who trusts you for a year is worth more than a buyer who feels manipulated today.
