### The Prompt Design

**Content Type:** ☐ Personal (most likely for sensitive topics) ☐ Contrarian ☐ Analytical

**Hook Architecture chosen:** _______________
**Why this hook (and NOT the others):**

_______________________________________________________________
*(e.g., "Vulnerability Ledger, because the reader needs to feel I've been in the trench. NOT Contrarian Cliffhanger, because that would feel like I'm exploiting pain for clicks.")*

**Narrative Arc:**

_______________________________________________________________
*(e.g., "Struggle → lowest moment → small realization (NOT a 'fix') → what I do differently now → invitation, not prescription.")*

**Additional constraints for sensitivity:**

```
SENSITIVITY CONSTRAINTS:
- Do NOT use the word "optimize" or any productivity-jargon.
- Do NOT offer a "5-step solution." This is not a framework post.
- Do NOT use the reader's pain as a hook to sell them a method.
- Frame any lesson as "here's what worked for me," NEVER as
  "here's what you should do."
- Allow the post to sit in discomfort. Not every beat needs a
  resolution. It's okay to say "I don't have a clean answer."
- The close should be an INVITATION, not a prescription.
  "If this landed, I'd love to hear your version." Not
  "Try this and let me know how it goes."
- Tone: [specific register, e.g., "quiet, honest, slightly raw.
  Like a voice memo sent to a friend at 11pm. NOT a keynote."]
```

**Emotional peak instruction:**

```
EMOTIONAL PEAK:
In [tweet 4 / slide 4 / paragraph 3], include a moment of genuine
uncertainty or self-doubt. NOT a performative "I was so wrong!"
beat. A real "I still don't know if I got this right" moment.
The reader should feel the weight of the topic, not just the
lesson extracted from it.
```

### The Test Run

Paste your full prompt (with all the above) and run it against a mock Hub for this topic.

**Read the output. Check:**

☐ Does the hook feel appropriate for the sensitivity level? (Not exploitative. Not clickbait. Not too light.)
☐ Does the arc allow for discomfort, or does it rush to a "solution"?
☐ Is the tone consistent with the register I specified?
☐ Would someone actually going through this topic feel *seen* by this post, or *marketed to*?
☐ Would I be comfortable publishing this under my name?
☐ Is there a moment where the AI got too "helpful" or "framework-y" and I need to pull it back?

> *This is the mark of advanced craft: knowing when the architecture serves the content, and when the content needs to break the architecture. The hook engines, the conditional logic, the narrative arcs—they're tools. You're the director. And sometimes the most magnetic thing you can do is let a post be quiet, uncertain, and unfinished. The AI can build the structure. Only you can decide when the structure should breathe.*
