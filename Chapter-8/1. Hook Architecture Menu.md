## 1. Hook Architecture Menu

**Purpose:** Replace vague instructions like “write a strong hook” with concrete, copy‑ready patterns your AI can follow. Pick two or three per platform and encode them into your prompt templates.

### Twitter/X Hook Architectures

| Pattern | Description | Example | Best for |
|---------|-------------|---------|----------|
| **The Bold Reframe** | Take a widely accepted belief and invert it in one sentence. | *“Most productivity advice is designed for people who already know what they want. That’s the mistake.”* | Contrarian content, thought leadership |
| **The Specificity Bomb** | Use an oddly precise number or detail that implies a bigger story. | *“I tracked every decision I made for thirty days. By day seventeen, I couldn’t choose what to eat for lunch.”* | Personal essays, data-driven pieces |
| **The Contrarian Cliffhanger** | State a controversial position and stop—let the thread deliver the evidence. | *“Decision fatigue isn’t real. At least, not the way we’ve been told.”* | Debate hooks, myth‑busting |
| **The Confession Opener** | A brief, vulnerable admission that makes the reader feel like they’re inside your head. | *“I spent six months thinking I had a motivation problem. I didn’t.”* | Personal stories, transformation arcs |

### LinkedIn Carousel / Post Hook Architectures

| Pattern | Description | Example |
|---------|-------------|---------|
| **Insight‑First Headline** | Name a specific professional struggle and hint at a framework solution. | *“The 3‑Step Framework I Use to Cut Daily Decisions by 60% (Without Losing Creative Spontaneity)”* |
| **Vulnerability Ledger** | Start with a costly personal mistake or failure that the post will then resolve. | *“Last year, I made a $15,000 decision while mentally depleted. Here’s the system that ensured I’d never do it again.”* |
| **The Diagnostic Question** | Ask a question that makes the reader self‑identify with the problem. | *“How many decisions did you make before 10 a.m. today? If you can’t answer, that’s the problem.”* |
| **Counterintuitive Stat Slide** | Open the carousel with a surprising data point that challenges a professional norm. | *“Judges are 65% less likely to grant parole right before a break. What does that say about your 4 p.m. decision quality?”* |

### Newsletter Subject Line / Hook Architectures

| Pattern | Description | Example |
|---------|-------------|---------|
| **The Curiosity Gap** | Imply a revelation without revealing it. | *“The moment I stopped trying to make good decisions (and got better results)”* |
| **Personal Time‑Stamp** | Ground the subject in a specific, relatable moment. | *“Last Tuesday, I sat down to write and… nothing came.”* |
| **The Controversial Promise** | Make a bold claim that the issue will back up. | *“Everything you’ve read about willpower is making you weaker.”* |

**Your assignment:** Circle the 2–3 patterns per platform that fit your voice and content mix. You’ll weave them into your prompts in Section 3.
