summarizing. You are building a tension arc that feels born on Twitter.

=== MY VOICE FINGERPRINT ===
[Paste condensed Fingerprint from Chapter 2]

=== MY TWITTER PLATFORM RULES (from my Playbook) ===
[Paste your Twitter AI Instruction Block from Tool 4, Section F]

=== HUB COMPONENTS ===
Core thesis: [Paste]
Story/Example for this thread: [Paste — pick ONE]
Objection to address: [Paste]
Quotable line: [Paste]
Data/proof: [Paste]

=== CREATIVE INTENT ===
This thread should make the reader feel: [e.g., "challenged in a
specific assumption they didn't know they held"]
The ONE takeaway: [e.g., "their 'time management problem' is
actually an energy management problem"]

=== STRUCTURAL REQUIREMENTS ===
- [X] tweets total
- Tweet 1: [Your hook pattern, stated as a rule. e.g., "Bold
  contrarian claim under 12 words. No question. No 'here's why.'
  Just the claim, like a door slamming."]
- Tweets 2–3: Escalate the tension. End each on a micro-hook.
- Tweets 4–5: Deliver the insight through the story. Show, don't tell.
- Tweet 6: Address the objection. "Now, I get it—" energy.
- Tweet 7: The quotable line. Give it its own tweet. Let it breathe.
- Tweet 8: [Your close pattern. e.g., "Reflective question. The
  reader answers it internally. No CTA. No link."]

=== FORMATTING ===
- Line breaks: [Your rule]
- Fragments: [Your rule]
- No hashtags. No "Thread 🧵". No links mid-thread.

OUTPUT: The numbered tweets. Then a [DIRECTOR'S NOTE] flagging
1–2 spots where I should add a personal touch or a timely reference.
```

ROLE: You are my structural adaptation engine. You are re-architecting

personal, too tangential, or too "inside baseball." It's a letter
to one person, not a broadcast.

=== MY VOICE FINGERPRINT ===
[Paste]

=== MY NEWSLETTER PLATFORM RULES ===
[Paste your Newsletter AI Instruction Block from Tool 4, Section F]

=== HUB COMPONENTS ===
Core thesis: [Paste]
Story to EXPAND (beyond what I said in the video): [Paste]
Original commentary I want layered in: [Write 2–3 sentences of YOUR
reflection that was NOT in the source. The AI builds around these.]
Quotable line: [Paste — weave in naturally, don't feature it]

=== CREATIVE INTENT ===
This section should feel like: [e.g., "the 11pm kitchen-table
conversation where I admit the thing I couldn't say on camera"]
The reader should walk away feeling: [e.g., "less alone in their
specific struggle, and curious enough to hit reply"]

=== STRUCTURE ===
- Open with a personal, scene-setting moment (NOT "new video is up")
- Transition: "which made me think about…" bridge into the insight
- Body: Expand the story. Add the behind-the-scenes. The doubt.
  The tangent. The thing I cut for time.
- Layer in the original commentary naturally
- Close: A genuine question or invitation. "Hit reply and tell me
  if this landed. Or if I'm completely wrong. I want to know either way."

=== TONE ===
- Most conversational of all platforms
- Can meander slightly. It's a letter.
- Contractions. Asides. "Here's the thing I didn't say on camera…"
- Length: [X] words

OUTPUT: The newsletter section. Then [DIRECTOR'S NOTE] flagging
where to insert a specific memory, a reader's name, or a reference
only my audience would get.
```

### Prompt E: The "Is This a Translation or a Summary?" Audit

*Paste any platform asset and ask the AI to diagnose whether it's genuinely rearchitected or just reformatted.*

```
I'm going to paste a [platform] post. I need you to audit whether
this is a genuine PLATFORM-NATIVE TRANSLATION or just a SUMMARY
with platform formatting.

THE POST:
[Paste]

THE ORIGINAL SOURCE (the pillar content it was adapted from):
[Paste or summarize]

AUDIT CRITERIA:

1. HOOK TEST: Is the opening genuinely different from the source's
   opening? Or is it the same first line, trimmed? A translation
   changes the ENTRY POINT.

2. STRUCTURE TEST: Is the narrative ORDER different? A summary
   keeps the same sequence (point 1, point 2, point 3). A
   translation might start with point 3 as the hook, use point 1
   as the proof, and cut point 2 entirely.

3. EMPHASIS TEST: Does the post emphasize what THIS platform's
   audience cares about? Or does it emphasize what the original
   format emphasized?

4. ADDITION TEST: Does the post ADD anything that wasn't in the
   source? (A platform-specific framing, a new analogy, a
   different emotional angle.) A pure summary only subtracts.

5. NATIVE TEST: If I removed all branding, would a native user of
   this platform recognize it as "one of theirs"? Or does it read
   like a transplant?

For each criterion: PASS (genuine translation) or FAIL (summary
in disguise). If FAIL, explain specifically what would need to
change to make it a true translation.

Be direct. "This is a summary with line breaks" is a valid and
useful diagnosis.
```
