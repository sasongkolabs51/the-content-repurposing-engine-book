## Tool 2: Platform Grammar Quick-Reference Cards

**Purpose:** A cheat sheet capturing the core structural DNA of each major platform, based on the chapter. Keep this near your workspace or paste it into your Playbook as a starter reference. Add your own refinements as you discover what works for your audience.

### Twitter/X Thread Grammar Card

- **Hook:** Stand-alone, high-impact statement under 140 characters; creates curiosity or challenges a belief.
- **Body:** 5–7 tweets total. Each tweet is 1–3 sentences. End every tweet with a mini-hook that forces the reader to unfold the next.
- **Line Breaks:** Use single empty lines between sentences for rhythm; white space is part of the format.
- **Payoff:** Final tweet delivers resolution, a reflective question, or a call-to-action—never a generic link dump.
- **Voice notes:** Fragments, direct address, conversational asides. No corporate throat-clearing.

### LinkedIn Carousel Grammar Card

- **Title Slide (Slide 1):** Promise a specific outcome or framework (e.g., “The 3-Part System I Use to Avoid Burnout”).
- **Narrative Arc:** Problem/struggle → insight → framework steps → personal proof point → reflective prompt.
- **Slide Structure:** Each slide has one bold headline + 1–2 supporting sentences. Visuals support the headline, not the text.
- **Length:** 7–10 slides optimal. Too short feels shallow; too long loses engagement.
- **Tone:** Professional yet personal. Blend insight with vulnerability (one “I failed” moment per carousel is powerful).
- **Close:** End with a question that invites comments and debate.

### TikTok / Short-Form Video Script Grammar Card

- **Hook (0–1.5 sec):** Combined text overlay + spoken line. Often a bold claim, a surprising stat, or a visual pattern interrupt. The viewer must *see* something change instantly.
- **Body Rhythm:** Short, staccato sentences. Every 5–7 seconds, change what’s on screen (cut to B-roll, text pop-up, camera move, graphic).
- **Text Overlays:** Script must specify exactly when and where they appear. They punctuate key points and act as second hooks.
- **Retention Mechanics:** Open loops, countdowns (“three reasons why…”), visual “chapter markers.”
- **Length:** 30–60 seconds for most educational content; can extend to 90 seconds if value density is high.
- **Close:** Cliffhanger, “follow for part 2,” or a quick CTA that doesn’t kill momentum.

### Newsletter Grammar Card

- **Subject Line:** Promise a specific story, insight, or behind-the-scenes moment. Curiosity with clarity.
- **Opening Paragraph:** Personal, scene-based, or a relatable problem. Establish why this matters right now.
- **Body:** Expand on the pillar idea with original commentary, anecdotes, or curated links. Deeper than social—this is where readers want substance.
- **Paragraph Length:** Vary rhythm. Long paragraphs are fine here; the reader opted in. Still break for scannability.
- **Tone:** Conversational, as if writing to a single friend. Signature phrases and voice fingerprints go here freely.
- **Close:** Return to the reader—ask a question, invite a reply, or tee up the next issue.
