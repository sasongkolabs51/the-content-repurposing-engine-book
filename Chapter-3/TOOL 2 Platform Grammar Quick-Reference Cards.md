## TOOL 2: Platform Grammar Quick-Reference Cards

**Purpose:** One card per platform. Tape them to your wall, set them as desktop backgrounds, or print them on index cards. These are the structural DNA rules you'll internalize until they become reflex.

---

### 🐦 TWITTER / X THREAD — Grammar Card

**The metaphor:** A fast-moving river. The reader is swept forward by current. Stop the current, they step out.

| Element | The Rule |
|---|---|
| **Hook (Tweet 1)** | Standalone. Must work if the reader NEVER clicks "more." Bold claim, counterintuitive reveal, or open loop. Under 280 chars. Implies a larger story. NO "Thread 🧵" announcements. |
| **Tension arc (Tweets 2–n-1)** | Each tweet ends on a micro-hook: a question, a "but here's the thing," a surprising stat, an unfinished thought. The reader MUST need the next tweet. |
| **Sentence length** | 1–2 sentences per tweet. Fragments welcome. Vary: short punch → longer explanation → one-word emphasis. |
| **Line breaks** | Single empty line between sentences within a tweet. Creates visual breathing room in the feed. |
| **Insight density** | One idea per tweet. Never two. If you have two, that's two tweets. |
| **Close (final tweet)** | Payoff. Reflective question, callback to the hook, or a quiet one-liner that lands. NOT "Follow for more." NOT a link (unless the whole thread builds to it organically). |
| **Length** | 6–10 tweets sweet spot. Under 6 feels thin. Over 12 loses momentum. |
| **Never do** | Hashtags. "In today's world…" openers. Summarizing a video ("In my latest video I talk about…"). Uniform sentence length across all tweets. |

### 💼 LINKEDIN CAROUSEL / POST — Grammar Card

**The metaphor:** A professional library. The reader is browsing between meetings, looking for one insight that makes them feel smarter or less alone in their work struggle.

| Element | The Rule |
|---|---|
| **Hook (Slide 1 / Line 1)** | A diagnostic question, a surprising professional reframe, or a vulnerability admission. "When's the last time you…?" or "I made a $40K mistake because of this." NOT "In my latest video…" |
| **Narrative arc** | Problem → Personal story → Reframe/Insight → Framework or steps → Reflective close. It's a *lesson wrapped in a story*, not a list of tips. |
| **Slide structure** | One idea per slide. Bold headline (6–10 words) + 1–2 supporting sentences. Max 3 sentences per slide. |
| **Tone** | Professional-peer. Warm but not performative. You're a colleague sharing a hard-won lesson at a conference, not a guru on a stage. Vulnerability is currency here—one honest admission per carousel. |
| **Visual rhythm** | Consistent design. Each slide should feel like a "page" in a mini-book. Use whitespace. Bold the key phrase. |
| **Close (final slide)** | A genuine engagement question. "Which of these three is your blind spot?" or "I'd love to hear how you handle this." NOT "Like and repost for reach." |
| **Length** | 6–10 slides. Under 6 feels thin. Over 12 loses attention. |
| **Never do** | Generic motivational quotes. "Agree?" as a closer. Walls of text on a single slide. Sounding like a corporate press release. |

### 🎵 TIKTOK / REELS / SHORTS — Grammar Card

**The metaphor:** A carnival. The reader's thumb is on a hair-trigger. You have 1.5 seconds to make them stay. Everything after that is a sequence of small "don't leave yet" moments.

| Element | The Rule |
|---|---|
| **Hook (0–1.5 sec)** | VISUAL first. Text overlay, a prop, a physical gesture, a jump cut mid-action. The thumb stops because of what they SEE, not what they hear. Then the spoken hook lands. |
| **Spoken hook (1.5–4 sec)** | Direct address. Pattern interrupt. "Stop doing X." or "Nobody talks about this, but…" or mid-sentence drop-in. NOT "Hey guys, so today…" |
| **Body rhythm** | Staccato. Short sentences. Something changes on screen every 5–7 seconds (cut, zoom, text overlay, B-roll, graphic). One idea per "beat." |
| **Text overlays** | Key phrases appear on screen synced to when you say them. Not subtitles—emphasis. 3–5 words max per overlay. |
| **Pattern interrupts** | At least 2 in a 45-sec video. A tone shift, a sudden zoom, a "wait—" moment, a cut to a different angle. Prevents the hypnotic scroll-away. |
| **Delivery** | High energy. Lean in. Use hands. Break the fourth wall. Talk to ONE person, not an audience. |
| **Close** | A one-line reframe that lands like a mic drop. OR a question that makes them rewatch. OR a cliffhanger ("Part 2 tomorrow"). NOT a generic "follow for more" unless it's earned. |
| **Length** | 30–60 seconds sweet spot. Under 20 for pure punch. Over 90 only if the narrative genuinely needs it. |
| **Never do** | Start with a logo intro. Talk for 10 seconds before the point. Use the same static frame the whole time. Sound like a YouTube video sped up. |

### ✉️ NEWSLETTER / EMAIL — Grammar Card

**The metaphor:** A living room conversation. The reader chose to be here. They made you a cup of tea (metaphorically). You have their full attention. Don't waste it with performance.

| Element | The Rule |
|---|---|
| **Subject line** | Curiosity + specificity. Hints at the story without giving it away. "The Tuesday I couldn't write a single word" > "My thoughts on creativity." |
| **Opening paragraph** | Personal. Scene-setting. Drop the reader into a moment. "Last Tuesday, I sat down at 9am with a full coffee and a blank doc. By 11, I'd written nothing. Here's what happened next." NOT "Hey! New video is up!" |
| **Body** | Expand beyond the pillar content. Add the "director's cut" thinking—the aside, the doubt, the connection to something else. This is where you say the things you cut from the video for time. |
| **Structure** | Thematic, not linear. One story → reflection → connection to the reader's life → insight. Can meander slightly. It's a letter, not a report. |
| **Tone** | Conversational. One reader. You're writing to a specific person (even if you have 10,000 subscribers). Contractions. Asides. "Here's the thing I didn't say on camera…" |
| **Close** | A genuine question or invitation. "Hit reply and tell me if this landed." Or a quiet sign-off that echoes the opening. NOT "Share this with a friend!" |
| **Length** | 300–600 words for a section. 800–1200 for a full issue. Depth is the point. |
| **Never do** | "In this issue:" table of contents. Corporate sign-offs. Summarizing the video with timestamps. Making it feel like a content digest rather than a letter. |
