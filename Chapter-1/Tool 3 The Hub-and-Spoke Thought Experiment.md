## Tool 3: The Hub-and-Spoke Thought Experiment

**Purpose:** To physically experience the difference between working from a wall of text and working from modular intellectual components. This builds the neural pathway for the Hub structure you’ll construct formally in Chapter 4.

**Instructions:** Take one of the pillar pieces you audited in Tool 1. Without looking at the transcript or the original notes, fill in the boxes below. If you get stuck, that’s data—you’ll see exactly where your raw material is still an undifferentiated blob.

### Pillar Content Title: ______

**Core Thesis** (in one sentence—what’s the single argument the whole piece makes?)  
  
  

**Supporting Story #1** (a narrative, case study, or analogy you used)  
  

**Supporting Story #2** (another distinct story, perhaps a personal one)  
  

**Audience Objection You Addressed** (what pushback did you anticipate or respond to? e.g., “But I don’t have time,” “That works for big creators, not me.”)  
  

**Most Quotable Sentence** (the line that, if someone remembered nothing else, you’d want them to repeat)  
  

**Now, imagine you’re building three Spokes from this Hub:**  
- A Twitter/X thread that opens with the quotable sentence and then builds a tension arc over 7 tweets. What’s tweet #1?  
- A LinkedIn carousel that reframes the thesis as a professional warning (e.g., “The hidden reason your content team is burning out”). What’s the slide 1 headline?  
- A TikTok/Reel script that starts with a visual metaphor (not your face talking). What’s the first 3 seconds? (Describe the action, not the words.)

Notice how much easier it feels to generate platform-native starts when you’re pulling from these discrete blocks rather than scanning a 3,000-word transcript. That’s the Hub-and-Spoke model in embryonic form.
