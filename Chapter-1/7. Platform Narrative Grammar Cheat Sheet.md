## 7. Platform Narrative Grammar Cheat Sheet

Keep this reference handy until editorial transformation becomes second nature. Each platform has a distinct “grammar”—a set of audience expectations you can work with, not against.

| Platform | Audience mindset | Hook expectation | Narrative rhythm | Ideal closer | Notes |
|----------|------------------|------------------|------------------|--------------|-------|
| **Twitter/X** | Rapid scan, curiosity‑driven | Short, punchy, often counterintuitive | Tension loops every tweet, cliffhangers | Reflective question or surprising reversal | Threads thrive on pattern interrupts; don’t summarise |
| **LinkedIn** | Professional identity, “what’s in it for my career?” | Surprising stat or relatable professional struggle | Story → diagnostic questions → actionable framework | CTA that invites perspective‑sharing | Carousels should teach, not just list |
| **TikTok/Reels** | Entertainment + micro‑learning, very short attention | Visual shock, bold text overlay, immediate action | Hook → tension → release, rapid cuts | Loop‑able payoff, challenge or “watch again” cue | Don’t explain, show. Body language matters |
| **Newsletter** | Committed reader, looking for depth and personal connection | Personal story or provocative question | Build context, deliver insight, add nuance | Gentle nudge to reflect or reply | Feels like a letter from a friend, not a blog post |
| **YouTube (long‑form)** | Lean‑back, ready for a journey | Open loop, stakes, “by the end you’ll…” | Chapters with mini‑payoffs, b‑roll stories | Satisfying resolution + future episode tease | Use storytelling, not just information transfer |
