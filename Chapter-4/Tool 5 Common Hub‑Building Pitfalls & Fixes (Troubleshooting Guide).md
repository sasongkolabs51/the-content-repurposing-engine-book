## Tool 5: Common Hub‑Building Pitfalls & Fixes (Troubleshooting Guide)

**Purpose:** A quick-reference to diagnose and fix the two most common problems that surface when creators build their first few Hubs.

### Pitfall 1: Over‑Condensed Stories (The “Bullet‑Point Ghost”)
**Symptom:** Your “Key Stories” block reads like a table of contents: “Story about the CEO,” “My burnout moment.” When you return to it two weeks later, you can’t remember the details and have to reopen the transcript.

**Fix:** After naming the story, immediately write 3–4 sentences that capture the setup, the moment of tension or turn, and the outcome. Use concrete details. Example:  
*Bad: “The CEO who batch‑planned Sundays.”*  
*Good: “A founder I coached moved all her daily decisions—meal prep, outfit, task prioritization—to Sunday evening. Within a week, she reclaimed 90 minutes of productive morning time and reported feeling less mentally drained by 10 a.m.”*

### Pitfall 2: Perfection Paralysis on the Core Thesis
**Symptom:** You’ve rewritten the Core Thesis five times, swapping adjectives and debating whether it should be two sentences or three. You’ve spent 20 minutes on one block.

**Fix:** The Core Thesis doesn’t need to be publication‑ready. It needs to be *directionally accurate*. Write a version that someone who hasn’t watched your content would understand. You will refine it naturally each time you feed it to a prompt and see how the AI interprets it. Treat the first draft as a working hypothesis, not a carved‑in‑stone statement.

### Pitfall 3: Formatting That Confuses AI
**Symptom:** You gave the AI your Hub and it pulled the wrong data point, or treated a quote as an instruction.

**Fix:**  
- Use consistent heading levels (all blocks at the same heading level, e.g., “##”).  
- Each quotable line and data point on its own line, preceded by a bullet or dash.  
- Avoid embedding instructions to yourself inside the blocks—use a separate section.  
- If an AI consistently misreads a block, try adding a short descriptive sentence right under the heading (e.g., “*Each bullet below is a standalone statistic with source.*”).
