## Tool 7: Complex Scenario Simulation – The Sensitive Topic Stress Test

**Purpose:** To practice applying the decision matrix when the content is emotionally charged, and you need to balance strategic scoring with ethical and authentic handling. This exercise integrates the matrix with advanced prompt craft from Chapter 8.

**Scenario:** You have a pillar piece titled “The Year I Almost Quit”—a deeply personal story about burnout, failure, and a slow return to creative work. It’s raw, honest, and hasn’t been repurposed yet because you’re afraid of coming across as exploiting your pain for engagement. The piece is evergreen (themes are timeless), resonates deeply (early comments from a small audience were intense), and has moderate shareability (the lessons are universal but the delivery is vulnerable).

### Step 2: Determine Platform Priority

Using your Metrics & Goals Map from Tool 1, compute the weighted score for LinkedIn (your primary lead‑generation platform). My calculation: (Resonance 3 × 0.5) + (Evergreen 3 × 0.3) + (Shareability 2 × 0.2) = 1.5 + 0.9 + 0.4 = 2.8. This piece is a strong candidate for a full LinkedIn carousel.

### Step 3: Design the Tone‑Guard Prompt

Your concern: you don’t want to sound like you’re leveraging your breakdown for clicks. Use the Conditional Prompting framework to add constraints. Fill in the prompt template below.

**PROMPT CONSTRAINTS FOR SENSITIVE TOPIC:**

- **Content Type:** Personal (forced). Do not use Analytical or Contrarian branches.
- **Hook Architecture:** Vulnerability Ledger, but with a “distance” modifier: “Share the story as something I’ve now integrated, not something I’m still in the middle of. Use past tense and phrases like ‘I look back now and realize…’ rather than raw present‑tense disclosure.”
- **Narrative Arc:** Struggle → Dark Moment → Small Turning Point → Ongoing Practice (not “I’m cured”) → Gentle Question for the Reader. Avoid any hint of a “happily ever after” or “3‑step fix.”
- **Tone Safeguards:**
  - No “you should” statements. Replace with “what I explored” or “what I’d offer if it resonates.”
  - No statistics on burnout prevalence to justify the story—it stands on its own.
  - End with an invitation, not a prescription: “If this resonates, I’d love to hear what you’ve been navigating.”
- **Voice Anchors:** Keep my core voice warmth, but increase fragments that mimic reflective thought (e.g., “So I stopped. Just… stopped.”).

### Step 4: Generate a Mock Carousel

Using a brief Hub (summarize the story in a few lines), run the prompt. Then assess the output:

- [ ] Does it feel honest but not exploitative?
- [ ] Does it avoid turning pain into a “lesson” too neatly?
- [ ] Would I feel comfortable sending this to someone who’s currently struggling?
- [ ] If a reader only sees this carousel and never buys anything from me, do I still feel good about the post?

**Adjust and iterate** until you feel confident. This simulation trains you to use the matrix for what it is—a compass, not a command—and to layer editorial judgment on top.
