## TOOL 2: The Three-Dimension Scoring Rubric

**Purpose:** Clear, specific definitions for each score level on each dimension. So you're not guessing what a "2" means vs. a "3." So your scoring is consistent across pieces and across time.

**Time needed:** 5 minutes to read. Then it's a permanent reference during scoring.

---

### Dimension 1: Evergreen Strength (E)

*How long will this content's core idea remain relevant and useful?*

| Score | Definition | Examples | Signals |
|---|---|---|---|
| **1** | Time-sensitive. Tied to a news event, a specific year, a trending topic, or a platform feature that may change. | "How to use Twitter's new algorithm in 2025." "My reaction to [this week's industry news]." "The tool I'm using this month." | Will feel dated within 3–6 months. Can't be repurposed next year without major rewriting. |
| **2** | Relevant for 6–12 months. Tied to a current season of your business or audience, but not permanently timeless. | "How I'm structuring my content business this quarter." "The framework I'm testing with clients right now." "Lessons from my first year of [X]." | Still useful next quarter. Might need a refresh in a year. |
| **3** | Perennially relevant. The core idea applies regardless of year, platform changes, or trends. | "The psychology of decision fatigue." "Why vulnerability builds trust." "The difference between busy and effective." | Could be repurposed in 2028 and still land. The insight is about human nature, not a tool or trend. |

**Quick test:** *Could I publish this same core idea two years from now with only minor updates?*
☐ Yes → Score 3
☐ Maybe, with some refresh → Score 2
☐ No, it'll be outdated → Score 1

---

### Dimension 2: Emotional Resonance (R)

*How strongly does this content make people FEEL? Not just "that's useful" but "that hit me in the chest."*

| Score | Definition | Examples | Signals |
|---|---|---|---|
| **1** | Primarily informational. Neutral tone. The reader learns something but doesn't feel much. | A step-by-step tutorial. A tool comparison. A data summary. | Comments say "Thanks, useful!" or "Great tips." Few personal stories in replies. Consumed and forgotten. |
| **2** | Contains a personal story, a relatable struggle, or a moment of recognition. The reader feels *seen*. | A framework wrapped in a personal anecdote. A "I used to believe X, then Y happened" narrative. | Comments say "This is so me" or "I needed to hear this today." Some personal replies. Shared in DMs. |
| **3** | Elicits strong emotion—vulnerability, righteous anger, hope, catharsis, grief, joy. The reader feels *moved*. | A burnout confession. A story of failure and rebuilding. A piece that names a pain people don't talk about. | Comments are paragraphs long. People share their own stories. Tags colleagues. "I cried reading this." DMs saying "thank you for saying this." |

**Quick test:** *When someone reads this, do they just nod, or do they feel something in their body?*
☐ They feel it in their body → Score 3
☐ They feel a recognition, a "yes, that's me" → Score 2
☐ They nod and move on → Score 1

---

### Dimension 3: Shareability (S)

*How likely is someone to share this with a person who DOESN'T already follow me? Does sharing it say something about the sharer?*

| Score | Definition | Examples | Signals |
|---|---|---|---|
| **1** | Interesting to my existing niche, but unlikely to spread beyond it. Sharing it doesn't signal much about the sharer. | A niche-specific tutorial. An inside-baseball industry observation. A tool review for a specific software. | Retweets/shares come mostly from existing followers. Few "I'm sharing this with my team" moments. |
| **2** | Broadly relevant. Could appeal to adjacent audiences. Sharing it signals "I'm thoughtful" or "I care about this topic." | A productivity insight that applies to any profession. A leadership lesson. A creativity principle. | Shares from people outside your immediate niche. "Sending this to a friend who needs it." Moderate cross-pollination. |
| **3** | Highly novel, counterintuitive, or emotionally powerful. Sharing it signals "I'm smart / in-the-know / empathetic / brave." | A surprising statistic that reframes a common belief. A counterintuitive framework with a memorable name. A vulnerability so honest it gives others permission. | High share-to-impression ratio. People share it as a social signal. "Everyone needs to read this." Goes beyond your niche. |

**Quick test:** *If a reader shared this on their own feed, what would it say about THEM?*
☐ "I'm brave / empathetic / in-the-know" → Score 3
☐ "I'm thoughtful / I care about growth" → Score 2
☐ "I follow this creator" (not much signal) → Score 1

---

### Scoring Consistency Check

After scoring 3–4 pieces, pause and ask:

☐ Am I using the full 1–3 range, or am I clustering everything at 2?
☐ Am I scoring based on the content's *inherent qualities*, or based on how well it *already performed*? (These are related but not identical. A piece can be inherently high-resonance but have underperformed due to bad timing.)
☐ Would I give the same scores if I re-read the piece cold, without remembering how I felt when I made it?

**If you're clustering at 2:** Push yourself. What would make this a 3? What would make it a 1? The distinction matters for prioritization.
